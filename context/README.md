## Youth Hackathon Overview

UNESCO Youth Hackathon empowers youth to become changemakers by proposing innovative solutions to pressing challenges, leading the way toward a more media and information literate world.
The Youth Hackathon is a flagship initiative of UNESCO’s Global Media and Information Literacy (MIL) Week. Each year, it brings together young innovators from around the world to learn, collaborate, and create practical solutions for a more informed and inclusive digital future. 

More than a competition, the Hackathon empowers youth as leaders and changemakers in today’s rapidly evolving information environment. Through mentorship, teamwork and creative problem-solving, participants design practical and scalable initiatives that address issues such as:
- Misinformation and disinformation
- Online hate speech
- Ethical digital engagement
- Critical thinking skills and media and information literacy

Since its inception, the Youth Hackathon has been successfully organized for seven consecutive years, fostering a growing global network of young MIL advocates, innovators, and community leaders. 

The record-breaking 2025 edition, under the theme *Youth Leading the Way: Building MIL Solutions for Impact*, attracted over 1,200 teams from 138 countries, demonstrating the power of young people to drive innovation and create impactful solutions for today’s rapidly evolving information environment.

## 2026 Hackathon Details

### Theme
✨ **Play Your Part: Youth Designing the Future of Media and Information Literacy (MIL)** ✨

### Timeline & Roadmap
1. **June 2026:** Registration opens.
2. **1 - 2 July 2026:** Two-day online mentoring programme.
3. **16 August 2026:** Submission window closes (final projects due).
4. **18 August - 13 September 2026:** International expert evaluation phase.
5. **Mid-September 2026:** Announcement of 4 winning teams.
6. **26–28 November 2026:** Voices European Festival of Journalism and Media Freedom in Thessaloniki, Greece (Winners showcase).

### Eligibility
- Teams of **2–6 members** (national, regional, or international)
- All team members must be **18–30 years old**
- Teams should align with UNESCO’s values, including peace, respect for diversity, and freedom of expression
- Gender balance and inclusion are highly encouraged
- No prior experience required—only passion and motivation!

### Challenge Tracks
Teams can choose one or more of the following focus areas:
- **AI and MIL:** Innovative solutions addressing challenges posed by AI through MIL.
- **MIL Education:** Creative approaches to MIL learning in the digital age.
- **Community Impact:** MIL-based interventions that empower communities.
- **Youth Engagement:** Strategies to position youth organizations as MIL change agents.
- **Open Track:** Other MIL-related ideas aligned with this year’s theme.

### Format & Submission Categories
Proposals can fit one of these categories:
- Games
- Applications/Websites
- Radio programmes/Podcasts
- Artistic work (comics, short videos, documentaries, etc.)
- Educational toolkits
- Youth organization campaigns
- Community-based interventions
- Other creative modalities for innovative interventions

### Submission Requirements
Each team must submit two components in English (under 10MB total):
1. **Proposal Document (PDF or Word format):**
   - Must include team members, problem statement, objectives, target audience, prototype/concept description, sustainability, creativity, and feasibility.
   - Must be clear, concise, well-structured, and readable.
2. **Pitch Video (max. 3 minutes):**
   - *Problem:* What problem are you solving, and why does it matter?
   - *Solution:* What is your project? How does it work? Who will benefit?
   - *Originality & Impact:* What makes your idea unique? What kind of impact will it have?

### Mentoring Programme
All registered participants gain access to a free two-day online mentoring programme (1-2 July) featuring global experts in MIL, digital transformation, AI, project development, and innovation to help strengthen ideas.
*Note:* Live session places are limited (first-come, first-served). Recordings and learning materials are available to all registered participants.

### Evaluation Criteria
An international panel of experts will review projects from **18 August to 13 September 2026**. Each project will be evaluated by **3 experts** to ensure a fair process, scoring on:
- **Consistency with the Theme:** Strong alignment with the 2026 theme and MIL principles.
- **Clarity of Presentation:** Clear communication, strong project presentation, and execution feasibility.
- **Innovation & Creativity:** Fresh, original, or inventive approaches.
- **Feasibility & Sustainability:** Realistic, resource-aware, long-term development potential.
- **Impact & Inclusion:** Addresses a real problem, promotes social good, and meets the needs of diverse or marginalized communities.

### Winners Reward
- **Four winning teams** will be selected in mid-September.
- Winners will be invited to showcase their projects at the **Voices Festival in Thessaloniki, Greece (November 26-28, 2026)**.
- **UNESCO will cover flight, accommodation, and meals for two (2) members** of each winning team for the trip to Greece.
- All participants who successfully submit a relevant project will receive a **Participation Certificate from UNESCO**.
