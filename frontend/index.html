Media Literacy Core Guidelines

To increase Media & Information Literacy (MIL) effectively, your fact-checks, campaigns, and reports should focus on the following guidelines:

  • Factual Rigor: Cross-reference all source claims laterally. Avoid relying only on visual elements.
  • Structural Clarity: Present claims objectively. Translate sensationalist clickbait headlines to neutral text.
  • Target Inclusion: Optimize media content accessibility for physically challenged and low-connectivity users.

Scientific Base Empirical Foundations & Research Library

Our MIL Nexus platform translates academic media studies and experimental findings into working digital prototypes. Explore the primary research foundations backing each module below:

🔍
Lateral Reading & Heuristics
Wineburg & McGrew (2019)

Demonstrates that expert fact-checkers evaluate unfamiliar sources by opening new tabs to cross-reference claims (lateral reading) rather than analyzing the site's layout. This backs our SpotCheck Kit verification flow.

⚖️
Civic Reasoning & Biases
Kahne & Bowyer (2017)

Investigates motivated reasoning and political polarization among youth. Finding that media literacy education mitigates these biases, backing our classroom guides and checklists.

🛡️
Psychological Inoculation
Roozenbeek & van der Linden (2019)

Exposing individuals to micro-doses of disinformation tactics (like deepfakes and clickbait) builds mental antibodies. Backs the active puzzles in our TruthCraft Game.

📢
Youth Participatory Politics
Mihailidis & Thevenin (2013)

Establishes media literacy as a core competency for active citizenship, shifting youth from protective consumers to active creators via production tools. Backs our Studio templates.

🌐
Civic Media Ecologies
Nichols & LeBlanc (2021)

Critiques representational literacy in favor of ecological mapping, examining platform layers (The Stack) and digital extraction. Backs our metadata and network tracking tools.

🌍
Cross-Cultural Prebunking
Roozenbeek, van der Linden & Nygren (2020)

A study of 5,061 participants proving that active gameplay interventions boost psychological immunity against fake news across diverse languages. Backs our TruthCraft layout.
